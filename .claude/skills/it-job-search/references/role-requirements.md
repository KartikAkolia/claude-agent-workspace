# IT Role Requirements & Market Data

Sourced per `docs/personal-research-guidelines.md` — official/authoritative sources only,
cross-checked, dated. This file is time-sensitive: wage, growth, and demand figures move
year to year. Re-verify against the live source before quoting a number older than ~12
months, don't just copy it forward.

## Field-wide outlook (source: U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook
Handbook, "Computer and Information Technology Occupations," data current as of the
research date 2026-08-30, most recently updated by BLS 2026-08-27)

- Fact: Employment in computer and IT occupations overall is projected to grow faster
  than the average for all occupations, 2025–2035, with about 280,000 openings a year on
  average (growth plus replacement of workers who permanently leave the field).
- Fact: Median annual wage for the group was $109,470 (May 2025) vs. $50,980 across all
  occupations.
- Fact, by occupation (May 2025 median wages, BLS):
  - Computer and Information Systems Managers: $175,140; employment +16% 2025–2035;
    ~53,500 openings/year.
  - Information Security Analysts: ~192,900 jobs held in 2025.
  - Computer Network Support Specialists: $76,220; Computer User Support Specialists:
    $61,860; overall computer support specialist employment projected -3% 2025–2035
    (structural decline in this sub-role even as the field grows), ~48,700 openings/year.
  - Computer and Information Research Scientists: $140,300; employment +22% 2025–2035;
    ~2,900 openings/year; typically requires a master's, though some federal roles accept
    a bachelor's.
- Source: <https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/>

## Industry-wide hiring trends (source: CompTIA, "State of the Tech Workforce 2026,"
released March 2026 — CompTIA is the IT industry's own trade/standards association, using
BLS + Lightcast labor-market data)

- Fact: CompTIA estimates 2026 net tech employment growth of 1.9% (~185,499 new jobs),
  rebounding from a 0.3% net decline in 2025.
- Fact: Tech-specific occupational growth forecast at 2.2% for 2026 (~128,000 jobs), and
  the tech workforce overall projected to grow roughly 2x the pace of the general U.S.
  workforce over 2026–2036.
- Fact: Fastest-growing tech occupations vs. the national occupational growth rate,
  2026–2036: Data Scientists/Analysts (+420% relative), Cybersecurity Analysts/Engineers
  (+346%), Software Developers/Engineers (+188%), CIOs/IT Directors/Managers (+175%),
  Software QA/Testers (+110%).
- Fact: In January 2026, 275,000+ active U.S. job postings referenced AI skills — either
  dedicated AI roles (AI engineer, AI architect) or AI-fluency requirements layered onto
  existing roles. Analysis (CompTIA's framing, not raw data): AI fluency is increasingly
  an expected layer on top of a base role rather than a separate track — worth naming as
  a skill on the resume even for non-AI-specialist roles, if genuinely true of the
  candidate.
- Fact: Median salary across tech occupation categories in the report: $112,805.
- Source: <https://www.comptia.org/en-us/resources/research/state-of-the-tech-workforce-2026/>
- Limitation to flag to the user: this is U.S. labor-market data. If the user is job
  searching outside the U.S., these figures are directional context at best, not a
  substitute for a local source.

## Entry-level and career-change certification path (source: CompTIA's own certification
program — CompTIA is the certifying body, so this is a primary source for what its own
certs cover and how they sequence)

- Fact: the standard sequence is CompTIA A+ (entry-level IT support: hardware,
  troubleshooting, networking basics, mobile devices, OS) → Network+ (networking
  fundamentals: protocols, configuration, security, troubleshooting) → Security+
  (cybersecurity fundamentals). Optional on-ramp before A+ for someone with zero IT
  background: CompTIA Tech+ (formerly ITF+).
- Fact: after the core sequence, specialization branches by goal — cybersecurity
  (Security+ → CySA+ or PenTest+ → SecurityX), or infrastructure/cloud/data tracks.
- Fact: A+ maps to entry titles like desktop support specialist, IT technician, field
  service technician.
- Fact: for U.S. federal/defense-contractor roles, DoD Directive 8570 maps specific
  CompTIA certs (A+, Network+, Security+, CASP+/SecurityX) to specific job roles —
  relevant only if the user is targeting that sector.
- Source: <https://www.comptia.org/en-us/blog/plot-your-next-move-with-the-new-comptia-career-roadmap/>
  and CompTIA's certification pages linked from <https://www.comptia.org/en-us/certifications/>.
- Don't recommend a certification path generically — match it to the target role(s)
  established during intake. A+/Network+/Security+ is specifically the support/sysadmin/
  security on-ramp, not a universal recommendation for e.g. someone targeting a data
  analyst or software developer role.
