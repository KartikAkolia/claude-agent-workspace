# Offer & Salary Negotiation

Sourced per `docs/personal-research-guidelines.md` from SHRM (Society for Human Resource
Management — the HR profession's own standards/professional body, so authoritative on
employer-side and negotiation-etiquette norms specifically).

## Before negotiating

- Fact/guidance: research market salary range for the specific role and location first
  (SHRM names Glassdoor and Payscale, alongside SHRM's own salary data, as free sources)
  — cross-reference against `role-requirements.md`'s BLS/CompTIA wage data for a
  field-wide sanity check, but use a role- and location-specific source as the actual
  number to negotiate from; BLS/CompTIA figures are field medians, not a substitute for a
  role-specific quote.
- Guidance: decide the walk-away number and any non-salary items that would offset a
  lower base (schedule flexibility, remote work, extra vacation) before the conversation
  starts, not during it.

## During negotiation (SHRM guidance)

- Lead with genuine interest in the role, then name the specific gap and the specific
  experience/skills that justify closing it — don't negotiate from a vague "I think I'm
  worth more."
- SHRM's suggested framing: "I'm really interested in the position. However, I was a
  little disappointed that the salary offer was lower than I expected, especially since
  I have [specific experience/skill]..."
- Limit back-and-forth — SHRM's guidance is that two counters is typically the practical
  ceiling; dragging negotiation out past that reads poorly to most employers.
- Total compensation, not just base: health coverage contribution, retirement match,
  vacation days, sign-on bonus, and an early (e.g., 6-month) performance review tied to a
  pay bump are all real negotiables SHRM names, and can offset a base number that won't
  move.
- SHRM's explicit caution: overconfidence reads badly — negotiate from researched facts
  about the role/market, not from a self-assessed sense of worth that isn't backed by
  comparable data.
- If the user has a competing offer, that's real leverage — SHRM notes multiple offers
  materially shift negotiating position; only use this if it's actually true, never
  suggest implying a competing offer that doesn't exist.

## Non-negotiable when applying this

This skill drafts negotiation scripts and counters for the user's own review — it never
sends an email, message, or reply to a recruiter/employer on the user's behalf. Offer
negotiation is outward-facing and effectively irreversible once sent, so always hand the
draft back to the user to send themselves rather than sending it directly.
